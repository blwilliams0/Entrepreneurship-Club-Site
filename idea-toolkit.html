The goal

Do not search for a perfect idea. Generate a lot of possibilities, improve the best ones, then choose something cheap and fast to test with real people.

Crazy 8s: fast idea generation

Why this has credibility

Crazy 8s is a core method in Google’s Design Sprint toolkit. The format is intentionally simple: eight distinct ideas in eight minutes. It is used to push past the first obvious ideas and create a wider range of options quickly.

  1. Fold the pageFold one blank sheet into 8 rectangles.
  2. Set 8 minutesYou get exactly one minute per box.
  3. Fill every boxSketch or write one distinct business, product, or service idea in each box. Keep moving even if an idea feels weird.
  4. Do not worry about artStick figures, boxes, arrows, and bullet points are enough. Concept over art.
  5. Circle the best 2Pick the two ideas that are most interesting, useful, realistic, or testable, not necessarily the most polished.
Prompt ideas if you freeze

What could I sell to students? What can I do with skills I already have? What could I start for less than $100? What annoying problem do I see repeatedly? What service could I perform manually before building software?

Source: Google Design Sprint Kit, Crazy 8s

SCAMPER: transform the best ideas

LetterPromptExample
S: SubstituteWhat material, channel, audience, packaging, or process can you swap?Put stickers inside a holiday ornament instead of a plastic sleeve.
C: CombineWhat two products, services, or experiences can you merge?Thrifted glass mug + cocoa mix + candy = ready-to-gift bundle.
A: AdaptWhat idea from another place, industry, or trend can you adapt?Adapt aesthetic desk-trinket trends for college dorm desks.
M: Modify / Magnify / MinifyWhat can you change in size, quantity, speed, quality, or format?Jumbo single cookie vs. mini 3-pack stocking stuffer.
P: Put to another useCan an existing object, skill, or process solve a different problem?Mini terracotta pots become desktop candy holders.
E: EliminateWhat cost, step, feature, or friction can you remove?Drop custom tags if they add cost but little value.
R: Reverse / RearrangeWhat happens if you flip the order or let the customer control part of the experience?Let buyers choose candy fills build-a-bag style.

Problem inventory: mine your own life

  • Write 10 things that annoyed you in the last seven days.
  • Write 10 things people around you complain about repeatedly.
  • Write 5 things people ask you for help with.
  • Write 5 tasks that take too long, are confusing, expensive, awkward, or boring.
  • Circle problems where you understand the customer well enough to find them and talk to them.
Useful signal

A problem is more interesting when people already spend time, money, effort, or emotional energy trying to solve it.

The $100 constraint

Constraint can create creativity

Ask: “If someone handed me $100 tonight and I had to turn it into a real business, what would I do?” You can use skills and tools you already have, but you cannot pretend you own expensive equipment, have free employees, or already have thousands of customers.

  • What would the $100 actually buy?
  • Who would be the first customer?
  • How would you reach that customer?
  • What would you charge?
  • Could you make the first sale within seven days?

How Might We…?

  • How might we help [specific customer] do [job] faster?
  • How might we make [painful task] cheaper or easier?
  • How might we turn something people already do manually into a service or tool?
  • How might we create a better experience for a narrow group instead of “everyone”?
  • How might we remove one frustrating step from an existing process?

Reverse brainstorming

Ask the opposite

Instead of “How do I make this business good?”, ask “How could I make this experience absolutely terrible?” List the worst choices. Then reverse them into opportunities.

Make it terribleReverse it into an idea
Make customers wait days for a reply.Offer fast response / guaranteed same-day quote.
Make pricing confusing.Use one clear package and visible price.
Make setup take an hour.Offer setup/delivery as part of the service.
Make the product hard to gift.Prepackage it as ready-to-give.

Choose what to test, not what to marry

Score each idea from 1 to 5 on every question.

Score 1–5Question
Customer pain / desireDo people care enough to act or pay?
AccessCan I easily find and talk to this customer?
SpeedCan I test this within a week?
CostCan I test it cheaply?
FitDo I have useful skills, credibility, or access?
EnergyWould I be willing to work on this long enough to learn?
Pick the idea with the best learning opportunity

The “winner” is not necessarily the biggest business. Pick the idea you can test quickly with a real customer. Evidence beats brainstorming.
